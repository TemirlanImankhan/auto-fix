1. Aim of the Work

The aim of the laboratory work is to study the subject area of the selected research, identify its main elements and the existing practical problem, and apply the methods of analysis, comparison, and classification to examine existing analogous solutions.

In accordance with the methodological guidelines, a subject area is understood as a part of the real world studied within a particular research or development project. It includes objects, processes, participants, data, existing solutions, and problems. A scientific or practical problem is a task for which a solution is unavailable, not fully developed, or characterised by essential limitations.

The following research methods are applied in this work:

  • analysis — dividing the subject area into separate elements (vehicles, symptoms, faults, users, data, information systems) and examining them individually;
  • synthesis — combining the obtained information into a comprehensive description of preliminary vehicle diagnosis as an intelligent information process;
  • comparison — identifying similarities and differences between five existing analogous solutions;
  • classification — grouping the selected analogues according to origin, target users, platform, and diagnostic approach.

2. Task 1. Selection of the Research Topic

The preliminary topic of the diploma (research) project is formulated as follows:

Development of an intelligent web-based information system for preliminary diagnosis and prediction of automobile faults using machine learning methods (AutoFix AI).

The topic is related to the investigation and development of an information system and a software product in the field of automotive service. It corresponds to item 30 of the recommended topic list (“Information system for vehicle maintenance and service management”) and extends it with a research component: experimental comparison of machine-learning algorithms for multi-class classification of probable vehicle faults.

The project type is a research software project. The subject area is automotive service and artificial intelligence. The main technologies planned for the subsequent stages are Python, machine learning libraries, Django REST Framework, React, and PostgreSQL. The practical goal is to develop a web system that uses a trained model to provide a preliminary estimate of the most probable fault on the basis of vehicle characteristics and user-described symptoms. The system is not intended to replace professional mechanical diagnosis; it provides a prior assessment and a recommendation to visit a service station.

3. Task 2. Description of the Subject Area

The subject area of the research is the process of preliminary diagnosis of the technical condition of passenger cars. In real operation a driver usually observes only a limited set of signs: a difficult engine start, vibration, noise, overheating, increased fuel consumption, or an illuminated Check Engine indicator. The same sign may be produced by several systems. For example, a difficult start may be caused by the battery, spark plugs, the starter, the fuel system, sensors, or the ignition system. A conventional rule of the type “if the engine starts poorly, check the battery” cannot capture such many-to-many relations.

Professional diagnosis is performed by a mechanic and/or by connecting the vehicle to OBD-II equipment. These procedures are accurate but require a specialist, time, and, in many cases, paid diagnostic hardware and licensed software. Intelligent methods make it possible to analyse a combination of technical parameters and symptoms and to return a ranked hypothesis rather than a single rigid rule. This defines the boundary of the present research: not the replacement of a workshop scanner, but a web-based decision-support tool for the vehicle owner.

4. Task 3. Review of Existing Analogues

Five analogous solutions were selected. The set includes one domestic (CIS) software product widely used for vehicles typical of the regional fleet, one cloud knowledge system officially distributed in Kazakhstan, and three international products: a consumer mobile service and two professional workshop platforms. This composition satisfies the requirement to include at least one domestic and two international solutions and covers different classes of analogues (desktop program, cloud service, mobile application, professional diagnostic software, hardware-software complex).

4.1. OpenDiagPro (domestic CIS analogue)

OpenDiagPro is a computer program for professional diagnosis of electronic control systems of injection vehicles of Russian and foreign production. The OpenDiag project has been developed since 2010; a commercial version with ELM327 support appeared in 2012, and later J2534 adapters were added. The current line (version 1.8.x, updates in 2025–2026) remains actively maintained. The program is used in workshops, service stations, and by owners who have a K-line, ELM327, or J2534 adapter and a personal computer. It reads and clears diagnostic trouble codes, displays live parameters, tests actuators, and covers engine control, immobiliser, automatic transmission, electric power steering, airbags, ABS, climate, and related modules, with a strong focus on VAZ / GAZ / UAZ / ZAZ and additional modules (for example Daewoo/Chevrolet). OpenDiagPro is a specialised operational tool. It does not implement machine-learning prediction from user-described symptoms and does not provide a web-based personal cabinet. Its value for the present research is the regional coverage of vehicles that are common in CIS markets.

4.2. MotorData Professional (regional cloud analogue, Kazakhstan market)

MotorData Professional is a cloud-based technical information system for vehicle diagnostics, servicing, and repair, developed by JSC “Legion-Autodata” (Moscow; the company has specialised in automotive repair information since 1994). In Kazakhstan the product is distributed by the official reseller TOO “Motordata International”; licences are paid in tenge, and the documented vehicle coverage emphasises makes that are common in Kazakhstan and Central Asia (Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Russian, and selected European models). The system provides approximately 20,000 interactive wiring diagrams, 60,000 diagnostic trouble codes with causes and test procedures, 4,500 ECU pinouts, component locations, technical service bulletins, and a professional knowledge base. Target users are workshops, auto electricians, diagnostic technicians, and training centres. MotorData helps a specialist interpret an already obtained code; it is not a consumer web application that classifies faults from symptoms by an ML model. It is nevertheless an important analogue because it shows how structured fault knowledge can support diagnosis and because it has an official presence on the Kazakhstani market.

4.3. FIXD (international consumer analogue)

FIXD is a consumer diagnostic service developed by FIXD Automotive, Inc. (Atlanta, United States; founded in 2014). A proprietary Bluetooth OBD-II sensor is plugged into the vehicle (generally petrol cars from 1996 and diesel from 2008) and paired with a free iOS/Android application. The application translates thousands of diagnostic trouble codes into plain language, indicates issue severity and driving safety, provides maintenance reminders by make, model, and mileage, supports several family vehicles, and stores scan history. The optional FIXD Premium subscription adds repair cost estimates, a mechanic hotline, 24/7 “AI Mechanic” chat, and an Issue Forecast that aims to predict future problems. FIXD is the closest consumer analogue to AutoFix AI in terms of audience and presentation of results. Its limitations relative to the research task are the mandatory hardware sensor, the primary reliance on OBD codes rather than on a documented ML experiment with Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest, and the absence of an open web information system with a research-style comparison against a rule-based baseline.

4.4. Bosch ESI[tronic] (international professional analogue)

ESI[tronic] is professional diagnostic software of Robert Bosch GmbH (Germany) for dealerships, independent workshops, and technicians. Combined with Bosch KTS (or DCU) hardware, it offers coverage of about 130,000 vehicles and 150 brands. Core functions include reading and clearing fault codes, analysing actual values, testing actuators, service reset, component learning, guided troubleshooting (SIS), experience-based repair cases (EBR), Secure Diagnostic Access to protected vehicle data, original manufacturer documentation, and expanding support for electric, hybrid, and Tesla vehicles. ESI[tronic] is an operational workshop system of very high functional breadth. An AI assistant that searches Bosch knowledge for a fault code or keywords has been announced for a 2026 software update; even so, the product remains a licensed professional platform, not a publicly available ML classifier for owners. For AutoFix AI it is a benchmark of diagnostic completeness and of ethically careful wording of repair advice (guided procedures rather than a single categorical claim).

4.5. Autel MaxiSys (international professional analogue)

MaxiSys is the professional diagnostic family of Autel Intelligent Technology Corp., Ltd. (Shenzhen, China; founded in 2004). It is a hardware-software complex: an Android diagnostic tablet plus a vehicle communication interface. Typical functions are OE-level all-system diagnosis, bidirectional active tests, more than forty service procedures (oil reset, EPB, SAS, DPF, injector coding, and others), ECU coding and, on senior models, programming, topology mapping of modules, the MaxiFix community knowledge base, cloud storage of diagnostic reports, and Remote Expert. The company describes MaxiSys as a diagnostic expert system connected to Autel Cloud. Prices of flagship kits are measured in thousands of US dollars, which places the product outside the reach of an ordinary owner. MaxiSys demonstrates the upper bound of practical workshop diagnosis and the usefulness of storing diagnostic history, but it does not solve the research problem of comparing classical ML algorithms on symptom-and-parameter data inside a web information system.

An additional grouping by diagnostic method is useful for the research question. OpenDiagPro, Bosch ESI[tronic], Autel MaxiSys, and FIXD (in its basic mode) are primarily code- and parameter-oriented systems: they communicate with the vehicle and interpret DTCs or live data. MotorData Professional is a reference knowledge system that starts from a code or a component already identified by another tool. Only FIXD Premium and recent Bosch/Autel expert-system features approach automated prediction, and even there the prediction is not published as a reproducible comparison of Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest on a documented dataset. This classification directly motivates AutoFix AI as an experimental, web-based, symptom-oriented research system.

6. Task 5. Comparative Analysis of the Analogues

Five comparison criteria were defined so that they correspond to the purpose of AutoFix AI and to the characteristics of the selected solutions. The criteria follow from the research question (how effectively ML methods can predict probable faults from technical characteristics and symptoms) and from the planned web architecture.

Criterion 1. Fault indication from user-described symptoms without a DTC. Shows whether the owner can start diagnosis from observed signs (difficult start, vibration, overheating, and similar) rather than from a code already read by a scanner.

Criterion 2. Machine learning / AI-based prediction of the fault class. Records the presence of a trained predictive model or an AI assistant, as opposed to a static code library or a fixed rule table.

Criterion 3. Web-based access through a browser. Distinguishes cloud/web information systems from desktop, tablet, or mobile-only clients.

Criterion 4. Personal account with a vehicle register and diagnosis history. Reflects the information-system nature of the future project (users, cars, diagnosis records).

Criterion 5. Accessibility for ordinary vehicle owners without professional equipment. Combines cost, usability, and independence from workshop scanners or proprietary sensors.

In Table 4 the symbol “+” means that the characteristic is available in a form that matches the criterion; “–” means that it is not available; “±” means that it is available only partially (for example, an AI chat that still requires an OBD sensor, or a cloud account that is a licence rather than an owner’s diagnostic history).

6.1. Justification of the scores

OpenDiagPro receives “–” on criteria 1–4 because diagnosis starts from a physical connection to the ECU, not from a symptom form; there is no published ML model, no browser information system, and no personal web cabinet. Criterion 5 is “±”: a private owner can buy an inexpensive adapter, but the interface is technical and oriented to service work.

MotorData Professional is a browser/cloud system (“+” on criterion 3). Criterion 1 is “±” because the knowledge base describes causes and checks for a known DTC or component, not a multi-class prediction from a symptom questionnaire. There is no ML classifier (“–” on criterion 2). The account is a professional licence, not an owner’s garage (“±” on criterion 4). Ordinary drivers are not the target users (“–” on criterion 5).

FIXD is the only analogue clearly designed for ordinary owners (“+” on criterion 5) and stores scan history for several vehicles (“±” on criterion 4: history exists in the mobile app, not as a research-style web information system). Criteria 1 and 2 are “±”: the main path is OBD code translation; Premium adds AI Mechanic chat, manual diagnostic walkthroughs, and Issue Forecast, which are AI-assisted but are not an open experimental comparison of classification algorithms. The product is mobile-first (“–” on criterion 3) and requires the FIXD sensor, so it is accessible as a consumer device but not hardware-free.

Bosch ESI[tronic] offers guided troubleshooting and experience-based repair, therefore criterion 1 is “±”. An AI assistant searching Bosch documentation has been announced for 2026, and EBR is a large case base, so criterion 2 is “±” rather than a confirmed classical ML pipeline. The software is a licensed workshop application (“–” on 3, 4, and 5).

Autel MaxiSys documents the use of customer complaints together with codes, provides MaxiFix and an expert-system layer, and stores cloud reports for the workshop. This yields “±” on criteria 1, 2, and 4. It remains a high-cost tablet complex for technicians (“–” on 3 and 5).

6.2. Common characteristics

All five analogues belong to the automotive diagnostic domain and aim to reduce uncertainty about the technical condition of a vehicle. All of them are commercial (or freemium) products rather than open research prototypes. Four of the five rely, in the basic scenario, on diagnostic trouble codes or on a specialist who already knows which system to inspect. None of the analogues publishes a reproducible experiment that trains Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest on the same dataset, compares accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, and then deploys the best model through a REST API of a web information system. None of them is positioned as a preliminary forecast that must not replace a mechanic — although FIXD uses cautious consumer language, while Bosch and Autel address trained technicians.

6.3. Main differences

The analogues differ first of all in the user category: FIXD serves drivers; OpenDiagPro can be used by both a workshop and an advanced owner; MotorData, Bosch, and Autel serve professionals. They differ in platform: Windows desktop, cloud browser, smartphone, workshop PC plus KTS, Android diagnostic tablet. They differ in the depth of intervention in the vehicle: OpenDiagPro, Bosch, and Autel can perform actuator tests and service functions; FIXD mainly reads and explains codes; MotorData does not connect to the car at all. They also differ in cost and in geographic specialisation: OpenDiagPro and MotorData are adapted to the CIS/Kazakhstan fleet; Bosch and Autel target the global professional aftermarket; FIXD is strongest on the US consumer market.

6.4. Analogue with the broadest range of functions

If the range of functions is understood as professional diagnostic coverage (number of brands and models, service procedures, OEM documentation, coding, EV support), the broadest solutions are Bosch ESI[tronic] and Autel MaxiSys. Between the two, ESI[tronic] is stronger as an information and guided-repair environment, while MaxiSys is stronger as a portable all-in-one scan tool with bidirectional control and topology. If the range of functions is understood from the owner’s point of view (plain-language explanation, maintenance, multi-car overview, optional forecast), FIXD provides the broadest consumer package. For the present research the professional leaders are not transferable as a whole: their breadth depends on hardware, licences, and workshop workflows.

6.5. Limitations of the existing solutions

The analysis identified the following limitations that define the niche of AutoFix AI. First, consumer and professional scanners require specialised hardware (ELM327/K-line/J2534 adapters, FIXD sensor, Bosch KTS, Autel VCI), so diagnosis cannot start when the owner has only a description of symptoms and the known parameters of the car. Second, knowledge bases such as MotorData interpret a code that has already been obtained; they do not answer the owner’s initial question “which system is the likely cause?”. Third, AI features in FIXD, Bosch, and Autel are closed commercial services; they are not accompanied by an experimental table of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score for several algorithms, which is required by the research part of the diploma project. Fourth, there is no web information system that combines registration, a personal vehicle register, a symptom form, a stored probabilistic result, and a history of diagnoses for the owner. Fifth, professional platforms are too expensive and too complex for the stated target user of AutoFix AI. Sixth, existing products rarely compare a machine-learning model with a transparent rule-based baseline, although such a comparison is necessary to test the research hypothesis.

7. Task 6. Conclusion

The subject area is the preliminary diagnosis of passenger cars by means of intelligent information systems. The main problem is that an owner observes only isolated symptoms, while the same sign may correspond to several faults; professional tools need hardware and a specialist, and rule-based checklists are too rigid.

Five analogues were classified as a domestic CIS desktop scanner (OpenDiagPro), a regional cloud knowledge system sold in Kazakhstan (MotorData Professional), a US consumer mobile service (FIXD), and two international professional platforms (Bosch ESI[tronic] and Autel MaxiSys). Comparison by five criteria showed a shared reliance on trouble codes and commercial licences, and a gap in web-based, hardware-free, symptom-oriented ML prediction. Bosch and Autel offer the broadest workshop functions; FIXD is the most accessible for drivers. Useful features for AutoFix AI are FIXD’s plain-language result and scan history, MotorData’s structured fault knowledge, Bosch’s guided and experience-based repair, Autel’s stored reports, and OpenDiagPro’s relevance to the regional fleet. The future system should combine a web cabinet, a vehicle register, experimentally compared classifiers, a probabilistic recommendation, and a clear statement that the output does not replace professional diagnosis.
